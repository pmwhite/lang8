Courses

Each number is always the same course. Pick one, or take a new one at random. Your best score on each is kept on this device.

✕

How the course works

Ride from the top of the hill to the finish. Every trick you land clean scores on its own, and the run's score is what they add up to. Every course is laid out at random, and what is on it decides which tricks pay, so each one is a little puzzle. New (N) lays out another course; R starts this one over.

Riding

  • Steer with the left panel: where your thumb is along it is where he aims, the middle straight down the hill. Let go and he keeps going the way he is. (Keyboard: ← and →.)
  • Pointed down the hill he runs fast; across it, he slows. The banks at the edges turn him back in.

Tricks

  • Press the trick panel and he crouches. Drag to choose the trick: up a front flip, down a backflip, sideways a spin; farther for more. Let go and he jumps into it. (Keyboard: hold Space and tap arrows, each tap adding a turn.)
  • He jumps the moment you let go. On a kicker, the most air is at the lip: let go too early and he only hops up the ramp; a moment late, still fine; later, less.
  • The trick fits the air: he turns just fast enough to finish it before he lands. If the air is too short for it, he lands sketchy.
  • Each jump ahead has a tag over its lip saying what it favors. While you aim, the tags turn green and say what your trick would score there, or red and TOO BIG, and each ramp glows green where letting go lands the trick, with a bright line where the glow starts. Most of the ramp glows for a small trick, a sliver at the lip for a big one, and none of it for one too big. A halfpipe's walls glow the same way, over the heights where letting go lands it. When letting go right now would land it, a green ring circles him and the trick's name turns green.
  • Press the panel again in the air to grab (keyboard: X). The longer he holds it, the more it's worth.
  • After an odd number of half turns he rides switch (backwards), and so he does after an air straight up a halfpipe wall, until the same turns him back round.

Sketchy

A trick he doesn't finish before he lands, or a gap he comes up short of, is sketchy: he rides away, but it scores nothing.

What a trick is worth

It starts from a base, bigger for bigger tricks but less and less so:

Flips1: 600 · 2: 900 · 3: 1100
Spins180: 200 · 360: 350 · 540: 480 · 720: 580 · 900: 660 · 1080: 720
Grab300, plus up to 200 more the longer it's held (at least a quarter of the air)
Straight air100, off a kicker
Rail, box300 to get on, then 150 a second

Each part is multiplied by what the thing he flew off makes of it (below): it only ever adds, so what it doesn't favor is still worth its base. A flip and a spin together are worth ×1.2. Then:

Air×0.5 to ×1.6: seconds in the air ÷ 1.2
Landingstomped in the sweet spot ×1.25 · clean ×1 · overshot ×0.75 · cased short ×0.6
Switch×1.5, and it counts as a different trick
Flat×0.25 off flat ground or rollers
Repeat×0.5 each time he has done that trick before in the run

The same trick means the same flips and spins, each the same way round, with or without a grab, switch or not. A rail and a box are different things.

  • Kickers throw him up for flips. Some sections offer two or three lanes side by side.
  • Gaps are good for anything, but come up short and the trick is sketchy; there is a safer kicker beside each.
  • Halfpipes: ride up a wall to air out of it; the walls pump him along, so you can go up again and again.
  • Cliffs drop away for long, floaty airs: a place to grab.
  • Rails (teal) and boxes (coral): land on one, or ride into its low end, to grind it.
  • Rollers are flat ground for scoring: hops there count a quarter.

The park

Park (Tab) goes to a skatepark: the same kickers, gap, rails, boxes, and rollers, turned every which way on a flat floor inside a bowl, and the same tricks, scores, tags, and light. There is no hill, so he pushes along to a steady speed, and the steering turns him rather than aiming him: he can go any way. Ride up the bowl's walls to fly straight up off them and come back down the way you came (switch), as off a halfpipe's. There's no finish: the session goes on, and R starts it over.

Taste

The best runs suit each trick to where it's done, and don't repeat themselves: a double flip off a kicker, a spin in the pipe, a long grab off the cliff, a rail between. Big tricks pay less and less for each extra turn, so one that fits its air and lands stomped beats a bigger one that's sketchy. The finish screen shows every trick and its reasons, so you can see why a score came out high or low.
