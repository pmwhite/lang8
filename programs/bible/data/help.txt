= reading	Reading	book
== bar	The page and the bar
Smooth Bible shows one long page of Scripture. Scroll down to read on; chapters follow one another without a break, each with its number and the book’s name at its start.
The bar at the top shows where you are. Scrolling down tucks it away, and scrolling up brings it back. Its title, “John 3”, opens **Go To** ([[going#goto|Going places]]). {sym:magnifyingglass} opens **Search**, {sym:text.book.closed} opens the **Bookshelf** beside the text ([[bookshelf#beside|Reading beside the text]]), {sym:bookmark} opens the **Library**, and {sym:textformat.size} opens **Reading** settings.
== footnotes	Footnotes
A small letter in the text marks a footnote. Tap it to read the note. References in a note can be tapped to preview the passage.
== headings	Headings and references in them
Section headings are the translators’, not Scripture. Some name parallel passages, such as “(Mark 1:9–11)”; tap one to preview it without losing your place.
== place	Your place
Your place is kept as you read, and the app opens where you left it, at the same verse, even after you change the text size or the version.
== keys	With a keyboard
⌘L Go To · ⌘F Search · ⌘] and ⌘[ next and previous chapter · ⌘← back · ⌘⇧P Listen. Space scrolls a page down, and Shift-Space a page up.
In Practice, Return checks, and ⌘1 and ⌘2 choose **Not Yet** and **Knew It** ([[memory#practice|Practice]]).
?ios On iPad, hold ⌘ to see them.

= going	Going Places	arrow.right.circle
== goto	Go To
Tap the title in the bar to open Go To. Type a reference in its search field, such as “jn 3:16”, “ps 23” or “1 cor 13”, and choose the first row. Or tap a book, then a chapter.
Go To also holds your ribbons ([[ribbons#about|Ribbons]]), your reading plan ([[plans#about|Reading plans]]) and the chapters you read recently.
== back	Going back
After any jump, a capsule at the bottom left names where you were, such as “‹ Psalm 23”. Tap it to go back. It goes away by itself once you’ve read on.
== history	History
Go To’s Recent section lists the last few chapters you read. **Show All** opens History: every place you jumped to or stayed to read, by day. Tap one to go there, or swipe it away. **Clear** empties it.
== previews	Previews
A reference in a heading, a footnote, a note or a definition opens a preview of the passage over the page. Read it there, or tap **Open in Reader** to go to it.
== search	Search
{sym:magnifyingglass} opens Search. It finds verses containing all the words you type, as whole words: “faith hope love”. Put words in quotation marks to find a phrase, “born again”, and end a word with * to find words beginning with it, “bless*”. The buttons under the field limit the search to the Old Testament, the New Testament, or the book you’re reading. Typing a reference offers to go there.
In the Hebrew and Greek, type Greek or Hebrew, with or without accents and points (“αγαπη”, “שלום”), or the word in Latin letters as it sounds (“agape”, “shalom”). A word as the dictionary gives it finds every form of it, with a prefix or without; another form finds only itself ([[languages#original|The Hebrew and Greek]]).

= tray	Selecting Verses	hand.tap
== select	Selecting
Tap a verse to select it, and tap more verses to add them. The tray rises from the bottom with what you can do with them. Tap a selected verse again to unselect it, or {sym:xmark} to close the tray.
== highlight	Highlighting
Tap one of the five colors (yellow, green, blue, pink and purple) to highlight the selected verses. Tap the checked color, or {sym:circle.slash}, to remove a highlight.
== actions	The actions
**Copy** {sym:doc.on.doc} copies the verses with their reference. **Share** {sym:square.and.arrow.up} sends them on, and **Share as Image** in the share sheet makes a picture of them. **Bookmark** {sym:bookmark} keeps the verse in the Library. **Note** {sym:square.and.pencil} writes a note on it. **Study** {sym:character.book.closed} opens the original words ([[study#interlinear|Study]]). **Compare** shows the verses in every version ([[versions#compare|Compare]]). **Memorize** {sym:brain} adds them to Memory ([[memory#adding|Memory]]).
== words	Selecting words
Touch and hold a word (or double-tap it) to select words instead of verses. The menu offers **Study** for that word’s original, **Highlight** and **Note**, then the {device}’s own Copy, Look Up and Share.

= notes	Notes, Highlights and Bookmarks	note.text
== notes	Notes
A verse with a note shows {sym:note.text} in the text; tap it to read the note. References you write in a note (“Romans 8:28”, “jn 3:16”) become links. **Edit** changes it; it’s saved as you type.
== chapternotes	Notes on a whole chapter
In the Library’s Notes, {sym:square.and.pencil} at the bottom writes a note on the chapter you’re reading. It shows by the chapter’s number.
== library	The Library
{sym:bookmark} opens the Library: your **Highlights**, **Notes**, **Bookmarks** and what you **Learn**: passages by heart, Greek and Hebrew. Tap one to go to it, and swipe one away to delete it. The search field finds words in them, and {sym:arrow.up.arrow.down} sorts them in Bible order or newest first. On Highlights, the colored circles show one color at a time.
== export	Exporting
{sym:square.and.arrow.up} at the bottom left of the Library exports all your notes, highlights and bookmarks as one text file, to save or send anywhere.
== versions	In every version
Highlights, notes and bookmarks belong to the verse, so they show in every version that has it.

= ribbons	Ribbons	bookmark.fill
== about	What a ribbon is
A ribbon keeps a place you’re reading from, like the ribbons in a printed Bible: one for your morning reading, one for a book you’re reading with someone. You might have two or three.
== making	Making one
In Go To, tap **New Ribbon…**, or touch and hold the title in the bar and choose **New Ribbon Here…**. Give it a name and a color.
== carry	Reading with a ribbon
Tap a ribbon in Go To to open it. You’re taken to its place, and the ribbon is carried: it moves on as you read, and its glyph shows beside the title. When you jump somewhere else, the ribbon stays where you were. Touch and hold the title to leave it at a place, or to move another ribbon here.
== edit	Changing one
{sym:info.circle} beside a ribbon in Go To renames it, changes its color or deletes it. Swiping it also deletes it; your History still has the place.

= plans	Reading Plans	calendar
== about	Plans
A reading plan gives you a reading for each day: the Bible in a year, in order or in the order the events happened; M’Cheyne’s calendar of four chapters a day; the Old and New Testaments together; the New Testament in 90 days; Psalms and Proverbs in a month; the Gospels in 30 days; or a chapter a day through a book. In Go To, tap **Reading Plans**, choose one and tap **Start Plan**. You can follow several plans at once: start another from Reading Plans, and each has its own row in Go To.
== today	A day’s reading
Tap your plan in Go To to open today’s reading. It opens on a page of its own that holds only that day’s passages, one after another, each under its name, such as Genesis 12–14, Psalm 105 and Hebrews 11:8–19. The title shows the passage you’re in, with the day under it. **Done** at the top left takes you back to where you were reading before.
Everything works there as in the reader: select verses, highlight, write notes, study a word. A reference you open from it takes you to the reader, and the capsule at the bottom left brings you back to the day.
== mark	Marking it read
**Mark Read**, at the bottom right, marks the day as read; tap it again to unmark it. Nothing is marked for you: reading to the end or listening doesn’t mark it. At the end of the page, **Next** opens the next day.
Days you miss wait for you; nothing is lost. In the plan’s days ({sym:info.circle} on its row in Go To) you can open any day, or tap a day’s circle to mark it.
== listen	Listening to a day
In a day’s reading, {sym:textformat.size} **Listen** reads the day’s passages one after another, and stops at the day’s end.
== reminder	A daily reminder
In a plan’s days, turn on **Daily Reminder** to include that plan in one notification a day, at the time you choose; it names each plan you’ve turned it on for, and opens the first one’s day. Memory can add its reviews to the same notification ([[memory#reminder|Memory]]).

= versions	Versions and Compare	books.vertical
== choose	Choosing a version
In {sym:textformat.size} Reading settings, **Version** offers the Berean Standard Bible, the King James Version, the World English Bible, the American Standard Version, Young’s Literal Translation, and the English Standard Version; and, under Original Languages, the **Hebrew and Greek**: the Hebrew Bible and the Greek New Testament themselves ([[languages#original|The Hebrew and Greek]]). The page changes in place and keeps your verse. Bibles you’ve downloaded are listed under **From Your Bookshelf**, and **More Versions…** finds others ([[bookshelf#bibles|Bookshelf]]).
== esv	The ESV
The ESV is read online. Crossway allows a limited number of verses to be kept on the phone, so chapters you haven’t read recently need a connection.
== compare	Compare
Select verses and tap **Compare** to see them in every version, one under another, the Hebrew or Greek last (first, when it’s the version you’re reading).

= listening	Listening	headphones
== listen	Listen

The Berean Standard Bible is read by Bob Souer, Barry Hays or Jordan Gilbert, and the World English Bible by Winfred Henson; the ESV by David Cochran Heath. Other versions are read by the {device}’s voice. Choose in **Voice**. There is no recording of the Hebrew and Greek yet.
== offline	Without a connection

== speed	Speed
**Speed** reads slower, normally or faster.

= study	Study	character.book.closed
== interlinear	The original words
Select a verse and tap **Study**. **Words** shows the verse’s Greek or Hebrew word by word, each under its English, with its transliteration and grammar code. **English Order** follows the English; **Greek Order** or **Hebrew Order** shows the original’s own order. {sym:chevron.up} and {sym:chevron.down} move to the verse before or after.
== wordstudy	A word study
Tap a word in Words, or touch and hold a word in the text and choose **Study**. The word study shows the word as it stands, its dictionary form, its meaning in this verse (and, below it, the meaning and domain the UBS dictionaries give it here), its grammar in plain words, its definition, how often it occurs and how the BSB translates it. **Add to Note** puts the word and its meaning in the verse’s note.
== grammar	Grammar, definitions, the concordance
**Grammar** explains each part of the word’s form; tap a part for its note ([[languages#notes|Grammar notes]]). **Definition** gives the full dictionary entry. **Concordance** lists every verse with the word, by book; tap one to go there.
== related	Related words
**Related Words** lists words used in similar places, in the same testament and the other. **In the Same Domain** lists words whose meaning belongs to the same field as this one’s here, such as Love, Affection, Compassion.
== references	References and commentary
**References** lists cross references for the verse, most useful first; tap one to preview it. **Commentary** shows Matthew Henry’s Concise Commentary on the passage, or another commentary from your bookshelf; **More Commentaries…** finds others ([[bookshelf#commentaries|Bookshelf]]).
== beside	Study beside the text
Study opens beside the text ([[bookshelf#beside|Reading beside the text]]), on top of what is there, so the verse stays in sight. It shows the verse you chose; select another verse to study it. **Back** returns to what was beside the text before.

= bookshelf	Bookshelf	text.book.closed
== shelf	Your bookshelf
{sym:text.book.closed} on the bar opens the **Bookshelf**: Bibles, commentaries, dictionaries and books from the library of CrossWire Bible Society, kept on this {device} to read without a connection, the one you read last first among each kind. Tap one to read it beside the text. {sym:info.circle} shows its details; **Remove from Bookshelf** there deletes it from the {device}.
== beside	Reading beside the text
What you read from the bookshelf opens beside the text, so you can read both at once: on the right in a wide window, or under the text, which keeps scrolling above it.
?ios Under the text, drag the line at its top to make it taller or shorter, or down to put it away.
{sym:text.book.closed} on the bar puts it away and brings it back as you left it, at the same book and the same place. **Back** goes back to the bookshelf, to choose another.
?ios With a keyboard, ⌥⌘I puts it away and brings it back.
== full	Reading it on its own
{sym:arrow.up.left.and.arrow.down.right} at the top of what you read beside the text lets it fill the screen, to read a book or a commentary on its own. {sym:arrow.down.right.and.arrow.up.left} puts it beside the text again, and so does tapping a reference in it, to show that passage.
?ios Under the text, drag the line at its top all the way up to fill the screen.
A commentary filling the screen leads: the text follows the chapter you read in it, so it is there when the commentary goes beside it again.
== browse	Browse the Library
**Browse the Library** lists what CrossWire offers: **Bibles**, **Commentaries**, **Dictionaries** and **Books**, English first, then a row for each other language. Search looks through the titles and languages of all four. Tap one for its details: what it is, how large, its licence, and **Download**. Downloads go on while you read, one at a time, and wait if the connection is lost. **Check for Updates**, at the end of the list, fetches it again.
== bibles	Bibles
A Bible from the bookshelf is read like the others. Tap **Read** in its details, or choose it in Reading settings → Version, under **From Your Bookshelf**. Its chapters are numbered as the King James Version numbers them; a chapter it doesn’t have says so where it would be, and its details say what it lacks, such as the Old Testament.
== commentaries	Commentaries
A commentary beside the text follows your reading: it shows the chapter you’re reading, at the comment on the verse at the top of the page, and moves on as you do. Select a verse to see its comment. Tap a comment’s verses, such as “Verses 14–16”, to bring them to the top of the page. **Next Chapter** and **Previous Chapter** read ahead or back; it follows you again once you move on to another chapter.
When your bookshelf has a commentary, Study also shows a **Commentary** choice above the commentary on a verse: Matthew Henry’s Concise, or one of yours.
== others	Dictionaries and books
A dictionary lists its words; **Look Up a Word** finds one, or a Strong’s number such as G25. A devotional opens at **Today**. A book opens where you left it, its **Contents** a step back; **Next Part** and **Previous Part** turn its pages.
== unreadable	What can’t be read yet
Some of the library can’t be read here yet: collections of maps and pictures, modules locked with a key their publisher sells, and a few in older formats. Their details say why, and they can’t be downloaded.

= memory	Memory	brain
== adding	Choosing a passage
Select verses and tap **Memorize**, or in the Library’s Learn tap {sym:plus} to add a longer passage by reference, such as “Isaiah 52:13–53:12”. A passage keeps the version it was added in.
== practice	Practice
Tap a passage to practise it. **Full**, **Some**, **Letters** and **Blank** hide more of the words each time; tap a hidden word to see it. Say the passage, then **Check**. Or tap {sym:keyboard} and type each word’s first letter; a passage in the Hebrew and Greek has a keyboard of Greek or Hebrew letters, with no accents or points to type.
== parts	Long passages
A long passage is learned a verse or two at a time, each new part joined to the ones before, over several days.
== schedule	Reviews
After **Check**, choose **Not Yet** or **Knew It**. A passage you know comes back after a day, then three, then a week, and further apart each time, up to six months. **Knew It** after showing or missing a word keeps the passage where it was, rather than moving it on; **Not Yet** brings it back tomorrow. Missing days costs nothing; a passage waits for you.
== hearing	Listen and Echo
**Listen** reads the passage once. **Echo** reads a verse, then waits while you say it back. Neither is there for a passage in the Hebrew and Greek, which has no recording yet.
== reminder	A reminder
Turn on **Remind Me to Practise** at the end of the Library’s Learn for a notification on days something is due. It shares the plan’s reminder and time.

= languages	Greek and Hebrew	textformat.abc
== start	Starting
Choose **Greek** or **Hebrew** under Learn Greek and Hebrew on the Bookshelf, or under Languages in the Library’s Learn, or tap **Learn Greek** at the end of a verse’s Words in Study. Each language’s page has what there is to do, by skill: Letters, Words, Forms and Reading. Nothing is locked or in an order; use what helps.
== stream	New each day
New letters and words come into **Practice** by themselves, a few each day: the letters first, then the most common words alongside them. **New Each Day** on a language’s page sets how many (none, if you’d rather add your own); any you add yourself that day count among them.
== choose	Practising what you choose
**Practice** on a language’s page has what is due today, with today’s new. **Practice…** chooses for yourself: words, letters or forms (or Hebrew’s joined words); from those due, those you’re struggling with, new ones, those you know, all of them, your words of the chapter you’re reading, or the next new ones in line, to go further today; and how many. Practising something before it is due changes nothing, unless you answer **Not Yet**.
A card you answer **Not Yet** more often than **Knew It** lately is struggling. Each skill on the page shows how many are, and practises them in one tap. **Your Words** can show only those, or only those you know.
== alphabet	The alphabet
**The Alphabet** lists each letter with its name, its sound written out (b as in bat) and an example word. **Practise Reading** gives words made only of the letters you know, to read to yourself and check against their transliteration. **Sounds Written As**, at the end of the Alphabet, chooses how the sounds are written: Erasmian, reconstructed Koine or modern Greek; Israeli or Tiberian Hebrew. There are no recordings yet.
== words	Words
**Add Words** offers the next most common words, words related to ones you know, or the words of a chapter or passage you’re reading. Each word is practised in a verse, a different one each time: say what it means there, then Check to see the BSB’s words for it, what form it is in the verse, and others from its family you know. **About This Form** explains the form. Answer **Not Yet** or **Knew It**, on the same schedule as Memory. A word you already know can be marked **Known**.
In the Hebrew and Greek reader, a word you tap also offers **About This Form**, and **Practise This Chapter’s Words**: your cards for the words of the chapter you’re reading.
== reading	Reading the original
When **Hide What I Know** is on (under the words, in the Greek or Hebrew order in Study), the interlinear hides the English under words you know, and the transliteration once you know the alphabet. Tap a word to show what’s hidden; tap again for its word study. **Verses You Can Read** lists verses where you know every word; tap one to read it in the Hebrew and Greek. **Read in Greek** (or **Read in Hebrew**) opens the reader there.
== original	The Hebrew and Greek
In {sym:textformat.size} Reading settings → Version, **Hebrew and Greek** puts the original in the reader: the Hebrew Bible (the Leningrad Codex, read right to left, with its vowel points) and the Greek New Testament, verse by verse as the BSB numbers them, so your highlights, notes, ribbons and plans are all there.
Like a printed reader’s edition, a word you haven’t learned has a faint dotted line, and under its paragraph is a short list of such words with the BSB’s words for each there. Tap one for the word: its transliteration, its meaning there and its grammar, **Word Study** and **Learn This Word**. Tapping the text still selects verses.
**Glosses**, under Version, chooses which words are glossed: **Words I Haven’t Learned**, **Rare Words** (as printed reader’s editions do) or **None**. **Show Accents** shows the Hebrew’s cantillation marks. A small ° follows a word read as the margin reads it (the Qere); its popover gives the word as written (the Ketiv), and the Hebrew’s own verse number where it differs.
== forms	Forms
A word study’s **Forms** shows every form of the word found in the Bible, with how the BSB usually translates it and how often it occurs. Tap a form to see its verses.
== notes	Grammar notes
**Grammar Notes**, under Forms on a language’s page, are short notes on how Greek or Hebrew works: the cases, the tenses and stems, the article, the pronouns and more, each with its table and the forms of a word that shows it.
== drill	Practising forms
**Practise Forms** shows words in verses, a different one each time, and asks what form each is: the aorist, the genitive, the Piel. Say it, then **Check** for the form in plain words, and answer **Not Yet** or **Knew It**. Each kind of form comes back on the same schedule as your words; a few new ones are added each time, the commonest first.

= settings	Reading Settings	textformat.size
== text	Text
?textsize {sym:textformat.size} opens Reading settings. The slider sets the text size; it also follows the {device}’s own text size. Choose a theme (Auto follows the {device}’s light and dark), a font, the line spacing and the margins.
?!textsize {sym:textformat.size} opens Reading settings. The slider sets the text size. Choose a theme (Auto follows the {device}’s light and dark), a font, the line spacing and the margins.
== show	What’s shown
**Verse Numbers**, **Section Headings**, **Footnote Marks**, **Words of Jesus in Red** and **One Verse per Line** can each be turned on or off. In the Hebrew and Greek, **Glosses** and **Show Accents** are under Version ([[languages#original|The Hebrew and Greek]]).
== screen	Keep Screen On
**Keep Screen On** stops the screen dimming while you read. It turns off as usual after ten minutes without scrolling.
== help	This manual
This manual is in {sym:textformat.size} Reading settings, under **Help**.
== about	Acknowledgements and privacy
**Acknowledgements** names the sources of the texts, recordings and language data, with their licences. Smooth Bible collects nothing about you.
