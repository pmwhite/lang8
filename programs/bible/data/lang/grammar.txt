# The terms of the tables' parsings, explained plainly (Smooth Bible's
# Grammar screen). A line a term: its name as written mid-sentence, then
# other ways the parsings write it (lower case, "-" and "/" as spaces), each
# after a |; a tab; and one plain sentence. Written for this app (public
# domain, as the rest of its data).
verb	A word for an action or a state: what someone does, or what happens.
noun	A word that names a person, a place, a thing or an idea.
proper noun|noun proper	The name of a particular person, place or people.
proper	Part of a name: this word names a particular person, place or people.
adjective	A word that describes a noun, telling what kind or which one.
adverb	A word that tells how, when, where or how much something is done.
pronoun	A word that stands in for a noun already known, such as he, they or this.
personal pronoun|personal possessive pronoun|personal / possessive pronoun	A word for a person, such as he or them, or for whose something is, such as his.
relative pronoun|personal relative pronoun	A word such as who, which or that, which joins a description to a noun.
reciprocal pronoun|personal reciprocal pronoun	A word for one another.
demonstrative pronoun	A word that points something out, such as this or that.
interrogative pronoun|interrogative indefinite pronoun	A word that asks who or what, or speaks of someone or something not named.
reflexive pronoun	A word that points back to the one doing the action, such as himself.
relative	A word such as who, which or that, which joins a description to what came before.
preposition	A small word that shows how one thing relates to another, such as in, on or to.
conjunction	A word that joins words or sentences, such as and, but or because.
particle	A small word that adds a shade of meaning but does not change form.
negative particle	A small word that makes a statement negative, such as not or no.
interrogative|interrogative particle	A word that turns a sentence into a question.
interjection	A word on its own for a cry or a feeling, such as oh or behold.
article	The word the: it points to something definite or already known.
number|cardinal number	A word for how many, such as three or a hundred.
ordinal	A word for the place in a series, such as first or third.
comparative	The form of a describing word that compares two, such as greater.
superlative	The form of a describing word for the most of all, such as greatest.
indeclinable|indec	A word, often a foreign name, whose ending never changes.
present	Shows an action as going on or repeated, usually now.
imperfect	In Greek, an action going on or repeated in the past; in Hebrew, an action not yet complete, often in the future.
future	An action that will happen.
aorist	Presents an action simply as a whole, usually in the past, without describing its progress.
perfect	A completed action whose result still stands; in Hebrew, often simply a past action.
pluperfect	A completed action whose result stood at some point in the past.
indicative	States something as a fact or asks a plain question.
subjunctive	Speaks of what may or could happen, often after words like if, so that or whenever.
optative	Expresses a wish or a polite possibility, such as may it be.
imperative	Gives a command or makes a request.
infinitive	The verb as a noun-like form, as in to love.
participle	A verb used as a describing word or a noun, often ending in -ing or -ed in English.
active	The subject does the action.
middle	The subject acts for itself or on itself, or takes part in the action.
passive	The subject receives the action: it is done to them.
middle or passive	The ending is shared by the middle and the passive; the setting shows which it is.
nominative	The case of the subject of a sentence: the one who acts or is described.
genitive	The case that shows belonging or source, often translated with of.
dative	The case of the one to or for whom something is done, or of the means.
accusative	The case of the object of an action: what or whom it is done to.
vocative	The case of someone spoken to directly.
1st person|first person	The speaker: I or we.
2nd person|second person	The one spoken to: you.
3rd person|third person	Someone or something spoken about: he, she, it or they.
singular	One person or thing.
plural	More than one person or thing.
dual	Two of something, such as two hands.
masculine	The grammatical gender of words for men and of many other words; it does not always mean male.
feminine	The grammatical gender of words for women and of many other words; it does not always mean female.
neuter	The grammatical gender of words that are neither masculine nor feminine.
common	The form is the same for masculine and feminine.
Qal	The plain, simple stem of a Hebrew verb, with its basic active meaning.
Qal passive|qalpass	The plain stem with a passive meaning: the action is done to the subject.
Qal passive participle|qalpassparticiple	A describing form of the plain stem with a passive meaning, as in written or blessed.
Niphal|nifal	A stem that is usually passive or reflexive: to be done, or to do to oneself.
Piel|pi	A stem that often makes the action stronger, repeated or deliberate, or that causes a state.
Pual	The passive of the Piel stem: the strengthened action is done to the subject.
Hiphil|hifil	A stem that usually means to cause someone or something to do the action.
Hophal|hofal	The passive of the Hiphil stem: to be caused to do or to be.
Hithpael|hitpael	A stem that is usually reflexive or mutual: to do to oneself or to one another.
Nithpael	A rare stem much like the Hithpael, reflexive or passive.
consecutive imperfect	A form joined to and that carries a story forward in the past: and he said.
consecutive perfect	A form joined to and that carries on a sequence, usually into the future or as a command.
conjunctive imperfect	An imperfect after the word and, carrying on what came before.
conjunctive perfect	A perfect after the word and, often carrying on a sequence into the future, like a consecutive perfect.
jussive	A softened command or a wish, often for someone else: let him do it, or may it be.
cohortative	A wish or resolve of the speaker: let me, or let us.
cohortative if contextual	Can express the speaker's wish or resolve (let me, let us), if the setting calls for it.
infinitive construct	The verb as a noun, often after a preposition, as in to go or when he went.
infinitive absolute	The verb's bare form, often used to strengthen another verb: surely, indeed.
construct	This noun is bound to the one after it, which is usually translated with of.
determinate	This word is definite, as with the; used of Aramaic nouns.
conjunctive waw|waw	The prefix and (also but, so, then), joined to the front of the word.
preposition b	The prefix in, with or by, joined to the front of the word.
preposition l	The prefix to or for, joined to the front of the word.
preposition k	The prefix like or as, joined to the front of the word.
preposition m	The prefix from or than, joined to the front of the word.
direct object marker	A Hebrew word with no English equivalent, marking the definite object of a verb.
pronominal suffix|suffix	An ending that adds a pronoun, such as my, your, his, or them.
paragogic nun	An extra n added to the end of a verb for emphasis or sound; it does not change the meaning.
punctuation	A mark in the Hebrew text, not a word.
